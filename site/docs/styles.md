# Styles

A deck's **style** decides its layout: the type, the spacing, how cards, charts and tables are
drawn, how much of the slide is filled. Its **brand** decides its colours and font. The two
are separate, so any brand works with any style, and you can change one without the other.

## How a style is chosen

You don't pick from a menu. When you ask for a deck, the agent:

1. uses the style you named (*"a board review in the folio style"*), or
2. your default style, if you've set one (*"always use grid"*), or
3. chooses the one that suits what you asked for, and tells you which, with a couple of
   alternatives: *"I used Folio because this is a board review. For something quieter, say
   'use grid'; for warmer, 'use bold'."*

To switch a finished deck, ask: *"make this deck bold"*. Your slides and your brand stay; only
the layout changes.

The styles are a guide, not a set of templates to fill in. Whatever the style, the agent
designs each slide for its content by the same principles: each slide makes one point, its
headline states the conclusion, there's one thing to look at first, and the closing slide
says what you need from the audience.

## The styles

Each picture shows the style in the colours it was designed with. With your brand, the
layout stays and the colours are yours.

### Grid

Quiet, precise and editorial: hairlines, square corners, mono labels, one accent per slide.
For board and quarterly reviews, reports, research and technical talks.

![Grid](/img/styles/grid.jpg)

### Bold

Warm and confident: heavy headlines, rounded tinted cards that fill the slide, one solid
accent surface. For pitches, launches, all-hands and workshops.

![Bold](/img/styles/bold.jpg)

### Folio

Editorial, like a well-set annual report: serif headlines, a margin rail with the section and
an italic page number, roman numerals. For board and operating reviews, strategy and research.

![Folio](/img/styles/folio.jpg)

### Signal

Technical and dense, like a good dashboard: mono headlines, panels with shared dividers, a
header bar. Dark when your brand is dark. For engineering reviews, architecture and metrics.

![Signal](/img/styles/signal.jpg)

### Atelier

Geometric and warm, like a design-school poster: flat circles, squares and arches carry the
numbers, colour panels bleed off the edge. For workshops, training and community talks.

![Atelier](/img/styles/atelier.jpg)

### Keynote

Photo first, words second: photos bleed to the edge, the type is big and there's very little
else on the slide. For kick-offs, all-hands, launches and customer stories.

![Keynote](/img/styles/keynote.jpg)

### Mono

Black, white and one red dot: light type, inverted blocks for what matters, calm and dense.
For design reviews, RFCs, proposals and pre-reads.

![Mono](/img/styles/mono.jpg)

### Sticker

Loud and chunky, like a startup pitch: boxes with thick borders and hard shadows, black-weight
headlines, one tilted sticker per slide. For investor and sales pitches.

![Sticker](/img/styles/sticker.jpg)

### Playful

Friendly and round: outlined chips, blobs behind portraits, an adventure-map path. For
teaching, schools, community groups and onboarding.

![Playful](/img/styles/playful.jpg)

### Classic

PPTD's original look: white cards with borders and a coloured bar along the top. Decks made
before styles existed look like this.

![Classic](/img/styles/classic.jpg)

## Photos

Most styles leave **photo placeholders** where a real picture would help: striped boxes with
a short note on what would work. Drop a photo onto one and it fills the space, cropped to fit
([more](./editing-by-hand.md#images-and-video)).
